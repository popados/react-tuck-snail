## Article outline

Answer the question in the first 50 words, prove it with the study, then give the reader something to do tomorrow. The product appears once, near the end, disclosed.

**H1: What Happens If You Miss a Day of a Habit?**

1. **Short answer** (100 words)
   - Open with a 40–50 word answer for the featured snippet: in the best-known study of habit formation, a single missed day had little effect on later progress.
   - One line on the catch: many misses do add up.
2. **H2: Why one missed day feels like failing** (150 words)
   - The familiar moment: a streak counter drops to zero and the whole habit feels undone.
   - All-or-nothing thinking turns one miss into "I've failed". Name "streak anxiety" and link to article #3.
3. **H2: What the research actually says** (350 words)
   - H3: The study. Phillippa Lally's UCL team recruited 96 people; 82 completed it. Each picked one new daily behaviour and reported how automatic it felt.
   - H3: 66 days is an average, not a deadline. Individuals ranged from 18 days to a predicted 254.
   - H3: One miss barely matters; many misses add up. A single missed day had little impact on later gains, but repeated misses lowered how automatic the habit became.
   - Caveat, quoted from UCL: very inconsistent people did not form habits, and the researchers did not know how much consistency is enough.
4. **H2: Why streak counters make it feel worse** (200 words)
   - A streak turns a habit into a number that can hit zero, so the loss feels bigger than 40 good days felt.
   - Keep this general; no figures without a source.
5. **H2: What to do the day after you miss** (300 words, numbered steps)
   1. Do a smaller version today: two minutes counts.
   2. Don't double up to "make it back".
   3. Avoid the second miss in a row (James Clear's "never miss twice").
   4. Note what got in the way, without judging it.
   5. If misses keep stacking, shrink the habit or plan a rest day in advance (a "shell day"). Link to article #6.
6. **H2: Track showing up, not streaks** (150 words)
   - Compare a broken chain with "12 of 30 days this month".
   - Disclosed mention: "We make Tuck Check-in, which counts days you showed up and never resets." Link to the pillar guide for other options.
7. **H2: FAQ** (200 words)
   - Q: Does missing one day break a habit?
   - Q: Is it okay to skip a day on purpose?
   - Q: What is the "never miss twice" rule?
   - Q: How many days can I miss before a habit fades? (Honest answer: the study does not give a number.)
   - Q: How long does it take to form a habit? (Short answer, then link to article #10.)
8. **Close** (50 words)
   - Tuck pull quote, lowercase: "missed a day? nothing broke. welcome back."
   - Care line: a habit app is not a substitute for care; link 988 for anyone in crisis.

## Competitive notes and differentiation

The results are thin: mostly short personal Substack notes, plus one long article from a competing app, so a sourced, kind explainer can rank quickly.

**What ranks now (checked Oct 12, 2026)**

- Personal newsletters such as [When you miss a day](https://notes.andrewnemr.com/p/when-you-miss-a-day): about 320 words, one heading, no research, good on self-compassion.
- [What to Do When You Break a Habit Streak (Habit Doom)](https://habitdoom.com/blog/what-to-do-when-break-habit-streak): about 1,900 words, four steps, a "what not to do" list and a 6-question FAQ. It cites Lally, Wendy Wood and BJ Fogg, uses a Reddit thread as an anecdote, and sells an app that locks your phone until habits are done.

**Match:** the steps, an FAQ, and the Lally study.

**Exceed with:**

- **The answer first.** Neither result answers the question in its opening lines; this one does in 50 words.
- **The study's own numbers.** 82 people finished, the 18–254 day range, and the caveat about inconsistent people, linked to UCL directly.
- **No lock, no punishment.** The competitor's fix is a forcing function; ours is a smaller habit and a planned rest day, which suits readers with anxiety or ADHD.
- **Honest disclosure.** Say Tuck Check-in is ours; the pillar guide lists other options, including paper.
- **Character.** One Tuck pull quote and the "shell day" idea make it shareable without making it cute.
