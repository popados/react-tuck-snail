# Topic Plan: Habits Without Streaks, wave two

Oct 19, 2026 · for the guides hub at `/guides/habit-tracker-without-streaks`

The hub's first ten articles are already planned in `docs/markdown/mascot-tuck-snail-topic-cluster.md` and wired in `website-skills/3-hub-design.md`. This plan adds the next ten (#11 to #20) so the hub keeps growing after the January 2027 article, without repeating a keyword the first wave owns.

**Audience:** adults 20 to 40 who have quit two or more habit apps, many with stress, low-energy days or irregular schedules, plus a developer slice that wants local or terminal tools (same as the content brief).

**How difficulty was judged:** from the results pages checked on Oct 19, 2026. No keyword-volume tool was used, so treat difficulty as a read of who ranks, not a number. Weak pages (Substack posts, app store listings, AlternativeTo pages) mean an opening.

**Voice:** studio voice in the articles, Tuck in pull quotes only. Say "days you showed up" and "shell day", disclose pocket@shell as ours, keep one free option in every comparison, and make no treatment claims. Articles #14 and #16 touch health-adjacent searches, so each carries the plain-text care note with the 988 link from the content brief.

## Topic details

### 11. Never Miss Twice: Does the Rule Hold Up?
- **Keyword:** never miss twice (also "two-day rule habit")
- **Intent / funnel / type / difficulty:** informational · TOFU · explainer · low
- **Why:** it is the most-shared "forgiving" habit rule, and readers who just missed a day search it next.
- **Angle:** results are Substack posts and personal notes that repeat the rule without a source. Check it against the UCL study: one miss barely mattered, but the rule still turns a second miss into a new failure line. Offer "days you showed up this month" as the gentler measure.
- **Key sections:** where the rule comes from · what the study actually found · when "never miss twice" helps and when it becomes a streak by another name · a monthly show-up target instead · FAQ

### 12. Lost a Long Streak? What to Do the Next Day
- **Keyword:** lost my streak (also "lost duolingo streak", "broke my streak")
- **Intent / funnel / type / difficulty:** informational · TOFU · how-to · medium (news sites and Android Authority rank for the Duolingo version)
- **Why:** the day after a broken streak is the exact moment people quit, and the moment the hub can catch them.
- **Angle:** app-neutral and kind. Results argue for or against Duolingo; this one is a next-day plan for any app, ending with the shell-day restart.
- **Key sections:** why a zero hurts more than 300 days felt good · what the lost number did and didn't measure · the next-day plan, one small check-in · decide whether to keep the app · FAQ

### 13. Rest Day Guilt: How to Rest Without Losing Progress
- **Keyword:** rest day guilt (also "feel guilty resting")
- **Intent / funnel / type / difficulty:** informational · TOFU · guide · low
- **Why:** this is the brand's first pillar, "rest counts", as a search people already make.
- **Angle:** results are fitness and wellness blogs about exercise rest. This one treats rest as part of any habit and introduces the planned shell day.
- **Key sections:** where rest guilt comes from · rest as part of habit formation, not a gap in it · planning a shell day ahead · logging rest as a check-in · Tuck pull quote: "some days are shell days."

### 14. Habits on Low-Energy Days: A Spoon Theory Approach
- **Keyword:** spoon theory habit tracker (also "habits on low energy days")
- **Intent / funnel / type / difficulty:** informational · TOFU · guide · low
- **Why:** people with chronic illness or fatigue are the readers streaks hurt most, as the content brief notes.
- **Angle:** results are forum threads, a Notion template and one app listing. Give a "smallest version" habit list and a tracker that counts days shown up. No health claims; care note and 988 link included.
- **Key sections:** what spoon theory is (credit Christine Miserandino) · a full-spoon and low-spoon version of each habit · tracking without a chain · when to rest instead · care note

### 15. Weekly Look-Back Questions (No Grades)
- **Keyword:** weekly reflection questions (also "weekly review template")
- **Intent / funnel / type / difficulty:** informational · MOFU · template · medium (productivity sites rank)
- **Why:** a weekly look-back is what replaces the streak as feedback, and it matches the look-back inside pocket@shell.
- **Angle:** productivity templates grade the week. This one asks curious questions and counts days shown up. Free copy-paste version plus a printable that pairs with #9.
- **Key sections:** why a look-back beats a counter · 8 questions, no grades · what to notice in mood and habits together · a free template · how pocket@shell shows the same week (disclosed)

### 16. Mood and Habit Tracker: Why Track Both Together
- **Keyword:** mood and habit tracker (a secondary keyword in the content brief)
- **Intent / funnel / type / difficulty:** commercial · MOFU · guide · medium
- **Why:** mood gives a low week context, so a quiet week reads as a hard week, not a failure.
- **Angle:** no ranking guide explains why pairing them helps; most results are app pages. Say "notice patterns", never "improve your mood". Care note included.
- **Key sections:** what each tracker misses alone · patterns worth noticing · what to look for in a combined tracker (privacy, no streaks) · options including a paper version and pocket@shell (disclosed)

### 17. Offline Habit Tracker: No Account, No Cloud
- **Keyword:** offline habit tracker (also "habit tracker no account")
- **Intent / funnel / type / difficulty:** commercial · MOFU · listicle · low (Product Hunt pages and F-Droid listings rank)
- **Why:** the "your data stays home" pillar as a habit search; #5 covers the mood side.
- **Angle:** the only list that checks each app's data claim the way #5 teaches (airplane-mode test, export, account prompt). Includes F-Droid options, tuckshell, pocket@shell (disclosed) and paper.
- **Key sections:** what "offline" should mean · a three-step check · 6 options with how each handles a missed day · export and backup

### 18. Cozy Habit Trackers: Gentle, Game-Like, No Grind
- **Keyword:** cozy habit tracker
- **Intent / funnel / type / difficulty:** commercial · MOFU · listicle · low (app listings and one trend page)
- **Why:** it is where cozy-game players look, and it is the hub's link into the games, which serves the "each product markets the others" goal.
- **Angle:** separates cozy look from cozy rules: a cute app can still punish a missed day. Ends with the games, where Tuck is the `@` and resting is a mechanic.
- **Key sections:** what makes a tracker cozy (rules, not art) · 5 to 6 options rated on missed-day handling · when gamified becomes grind · Tuck's games (link to `/games`) · FAQ

### 19. Finch Alternatives Without a Pet to Keep Alive
- **Keyword:** finch alternative
- **Intent / funnel / type / difficulty:** commercial · BOFU · comparison · medium (AlternativeTo and a competitor's list rank)
- **Why:** Finch users who feel guilty about a neglected pet are the closest audience to Tuck, who needs nothing from you.
- **Angle:** fair to Finch. Rank alternatives by what happens when you're away, not by features. Verify every app detail on its store page the week of publishing.
- **Key sections:** why people leave Finch · how each alternative treats an absence · comparison table · a mascot with no needs (pocket@shell, disclosed) · a free option

### 20. Daylio Alternatives: Private Mood Trackers
- **Keyword:** daylio alternative
- **Intent / funnel / type / difficulty:** commercial · BOFU · comparison · medium (AlternativeTo dominates; no strong guide)
- **Why:** it is the named-app search for the brand's mood-first competitor, and privacy is the reason people look.
- **Angle:** compare on where entries live, whether an account is needed and whether habits sit beside mood. Same honest disclosure and free option.
- **Key sections:** why people look beyond Daylio · what to check before you switch · comparison table · how to export your Daylio history · FAQ
